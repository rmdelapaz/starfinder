Playing Starfinder Solo

No GM and no crew: one character, a d20 and a log, with the dice deciding what waits on the next station or world.

Why Starfinder Works Alone

Starfinder is built for exploration: new worlds, derelict ships, stations full of people with agendas. That suits solo play, where you discover the story as you go. What's missing is someone to answer your questions, and that's the job of an oracle. You ask the yes-or-no questions a GM would answer ("Is the airlock sealed?"), and the dice decide.

Nothing on this page is from the rulebooks. The oracle, the scene check and the tables are tools for playing alone; anything that changes a rule is labelled house rule.

What you need

  • A character built with the normal rules (see Character Creation & Races), or one of the 315 Sample Characters.
  • A d20 and a d10, or the tool on this page and the Dice Roller.
  • A starting point: a job offer on Absalom Station, a distress call, a debt.
  • A log, or the session log below.

The Oracle

Ask a question that can be answered yes or no, decide how likely a "yes" is, and roll a d20 plus the likelihood modifier against DC 11, like any check:

  • Very likely: +6
  • Likely: +3
  • Toss-up: +0
  • Unlikely: −3
  • Very unlikely: −6

Then read the answer by how far the total lands from 11.

TotalAnswer
19 or more (beat it by 8+)Yes, and… You get it, plus something more.
14–18Yes.
11–13 (made it by 0–2)Yes, but… It's true, with a cost or a complication.
8–10 (missed by 1–3)No, but… Not that, but something useful.
3–7No.
2 or less (missed by 9+)No, and… Not that, and things get worse.

Twist: a natural 20 on the die adds something unexpected in your favour; a natural 1 adds something unexpected against you. Roll a spark if you need an idea.

Exact odds

A d20 has 20 equally likely faces, so every chance is a multiple of 5%. The tool on this page uses the same calculation.

LikelihoodYes, andYesYes, butNo, butNoNo, andAny yesTwist
Very likely (+6)40%25%15%15%5%0%80%10%
Likely (+3)25%25%15%15%20%0%65%10%

Very unlikely (−6)0%5%15%15%25%40%20%10%

Each step of likelihood moves the chance of a yes by 15%, and the table mirrors itself: "unlikely" is "likely" upside down. The twist column is the natural 20 and the natural 1 together, 5% each, whatever the likelihood.

Ask only questions where both answers would be interesting. If you already know the answer, or a "no" would stop the story dead, just decide.

Scenes, People and Sparks

Scene check

When you start a new scene, decide what you expect to happen, then roll a d20:

d20The scene is…Chance
1–2Interrupted: something else happens instead. Roll a spark.10%
3–5Altered: as expected, but one important detail is different. Ask the oracle what.15%
6–20As expected.75%

NPC attitude

When you meet someone whose attitude isn't obvious, roll a d20. The attitudes are the ones Diplomacy and Intimidate work with:

d20Attitude
1–2Hostile: wants you gone, robbed, or worse.
3–6Unfriendly: unhelpful and suspicious.
7–14Indifferent: busy with their own life; will deal if it's worth their time.
15–18Friendly: helps if it doesn't cost much.
19–20Helpful: on your side, maybe for reasons of their own.

Playing the GM's Part

Checks and fights

Your character's actions use the normal rules: checks against a DC, attacks against EAC or KAC. Set the DC before you roll. For opponents, decide their action by asking the oracle "Does it do the obvious thing?" at Likely; on a "no", roll a spark for what it does instead. Keep fights small: one character can't trade blows with a whole squad.

The threat clock

House rule: the threat clock. A GM keeps the opposition moving while you are busy; alone, a clock does it. Mark one step each time the oracle answers No, and… or rolls a natural 1, and each time you drop to 0 Stamina Points. When the clock reaches 4, clear it: the next scene is interrupted by whoever you've crossed, without a scene check.

A crew of one

  • A companion (house rule): one NPC, such as a drone, a pilot or a hired gun, run with a handful of numbers. They can make one check per scene you're not trained for.
  • Starships: a lone character can't fill every crew role. Treat starship fights as chases you escape with Piloting, or give the ship a crew of NPCs who act on the oracle's answers.
  • Resolve: use Resolve Points exactly as written. They are what keeps a solo character alive; spend them early rather than die holding them.

Solo Tool

Threat clock: 0 of 4

Session log

    The log is kept in this browser only.

    Your First Solo Session

    1. Make a character and a hook. One character and one reason to be somewhere risky: a contract, a debt, a signal from a world no one visits.
    2. Frame the first scene. One line: where you are and what you expect. Make a scene check.
    3. Play it. Ask the oracle what a GM would tell you. Make normal checks for anything your character does that could fail, and set the DC first.
    4. Track your resources. Stamina, Hit Points and Resolve matter more alone. Rest when the story allows it.
    5. Watch the threat clock. When it fills, whoever you've crossed comes looking for you.
    6. Stop after three or four scenes. Write one line about what's unresolved. That's next session's hook.

    An Example of Play

    Ilex Varo is a ysoki operative with a debt to a dockside fixer on Absalom Station. The fixer wants a data chip recovered from a cargo hauler that docked last night.

    Scene 1: the cargo bay. I expect a quiet bay and a single guard. Scene check: d20 shows 12, so it's as expected.

    Oracle: "Is the chip still aboard?" Likely, so +3. The d20 shows 14: a total of 17, beating 11 by 6, so Yes. Not a natural 20 or 1, so no twist.

    A real check. Ilex sneaks past the guard: Stealth +9 against a DC I set at 20. The d20 shows 13, for 22: she's past him and into the hold.

    Oracle: "Is the chip where the fixer said it would be?" Toss-up, +0. The d20 shows 1: a total of 1, missed by 10, so No, and…, plus a natural 1, a twist against her. The chip's case is there and empty, and the cargo manifest has been wiped from the hauler's computers. Someone else got here first and covered their tracks. The threat clock moves to 1 for the "No, and…" (a natural 1 on the same roll doesn't count twice).

    A fight. The guard heard something. Ilex fires her tactical semi-auto pistol: attack +6 against his KAC of 14. The d20 shows 9, for 15: a hit. The pistol's 1d6 piercing damage rolls 4, and the guard's 10 Stamina Points drop to 6.

    That's the loop: ask, roll, interpret, write a line, move on. Whether Ilex tells the fixer the truth is a question only you can answer.
