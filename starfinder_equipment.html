Equipment

How weapons, armor, upgrades and everyday gear work in Starfinder First Edition, and how to choose them.

Your Galactic Arsenal

In the dangerous universe of Starfinder, the right equipment can mean the difference between heroic victory and tragic defeat. Your gear isn't just statistics on a character sheet – it's your lifeline in hostile environments, your edge in combat, and your solution to countless problems. Think of equipment selection like choosing the perfect toolkit for a job, except your "job" involves exploring alien worlds, fighting cosmic horrors, and surviving in the vacuum of space.

The Professional Athlete Analogy

Selecting equipment in Starfinder is like outfitting a professional athlete for competition. A tennis player needs the right racquet, shoes, and strings for their playing style and the court conditions. Similarly, your character needs weapons suited to their combat style, armor appropriate for their role, and technology that complements their abilities. Just as an athlete's performance improves with better equipment, your character becomes more effective with appropriate gear. And just like sports equipment, Starfinder gear comes in different quality levels – from amateur starter gear to professional-grade equipment to legendary custom items that make champions.

How Gear Works: Item Level, Credits and Bulk

  • Item level: Every item has a level from 1 to 20. It tells you roughly when the item suits a character (compare it to your character level), and it also feeds into rules: a weapon's critical-effect or explosion save DC is 10 + ½ the item level + an ability modifier.
  • Credits: The galaxy's currency, held in digital accounts or on credsticks. A new 1st-level character has 1,000 credits to spend. Universal polymer base (UPB) is the raw material used to craft gear.
  • Bulk: Items have a Bulk number, "L" (light) or "—" (negligible). Ten L items count as 1 Bulk (drop fractions). Carrying more than half your Strength score in Bulk makes you encumbered, and you can't voluntarily carry more than your Strength score.
  • Selling: Loot you sell usually fetches only 10% of its price, so most of your wealth comes from mission pay and story rewards rather than from hauling gear to market.

Understanding Equipment Roles

Every piece of equipment falls into one or more functional categories:

Problem Solvers

Equipment that helps you overcome specific challenges or obstacles. Tools, computers, and specialized devices that expand what your character can accomplish.

  • Example: A hacking kit for getting past computer security
  • Selection Tip: Choose based on problems you expect to encounter

Force Multipliers

Equipment that makes your existing abilities more effective. Enhancement items, quality weapons, and augmentations that amplify your character's natural capabilities.

  • Example: A flaming weapon fusion that wreathes your weapon in starfire
  • Selection Tip: Enhance your strengths rather than covering weaknesses

Safety Nets

Equipment that protects you from failure or provides backup options. Armor, medical supplies, and emergency gear that keep you alive when things go wrong.

  • Example: Armor, because every suit has built-in environmental protections against vacuum and hostile atmospheres
  • Selection Tip: Prepare for the most dangerous scenarios first

Enablers

Equipment that allows you to attempt actions you couldn't otherwise perform. Vehicles for transportation, specialized tools for unique tasks, and technology that grants new capabilities.

  • Example: A starship for interplanetary travel
  • Selection Tip: Consider what new possibilities each item opens

Weapons - Your Instruments of Persuasion

Understanding Weapon Types

The Core Rulebook sorts weapons into proficiency categories: basic melee, advanced melee, small arms (one-handed guns), longarms and heavy weapons (two-handed), sniper weapons, grenades and special weapons, plus ammunition and solarian crystals. Your class decides which categories you're proficient with. Within those categories, the damage a weapon deals is either kinetic or energy, and that decides which of the target's two Armor Classes you attack:

  • Kinetic damage — bludgeoning (B), piercing (P), slashing (S) — targets KAC (Kinetic Armor Class).
  • Energy damage — acid (A), cold (C), electricity (E), fire (F), sonic (So) — targets EAC (Energy Armor Class) when the weapon deals only energy damage.
  • A weapon that mixes the two (such as a pulse gauntlet, B & So) targets KAC.

Kinetic Weapons - Classical Projectiles

These weapons use physical projectiles or blades and deal physical damage through impact and penetration. They attack the target's KAC.

Projectile Weapons

Firearms that use cartridges or other physical ammunition.

  • Advantages: Cheap, simple, available everywhere
  • Disadvantages: You track ammunition and reload; heavily armored foes often have high KAC
  • Examples: Tactical semi-auto pistol (item level 1, 1d6 P), rifles, machine guns
  • Best For: Characters who want proven, dependable weapons
Melee Weapons

Blades, batons and gauntlets in the basic and advanced melee categories. Some have the powered property: an internal battery that drains per minute of use rather than per attack.

  • Advantages: No ammunition for most, Strength adds to damage, simple to use
  • Disadvantages: You have to get close, and moving away can provoke attacks of opportunity
  • Examples: Tactical baton (1d4 B), tactical dueling sword (1d6 S), pulse gauntlets
  • Best For: Melee combatants such as soldiers, solarians and vesk

Energy Weapons - Pure Force

These weapons project lasers, plasma, electricity, sound or cold. Most draw on rechargeable batteries measured in charges, and if they deal only energy damage they attack the target's EAC.

Laser Weapons

Focused light beams that deal fire damage.

  • Advantages: Long range increments, cheap at low levels
  • Disadvantages: Battery charges run out; some creatures resist fire
  • Examples: Azimuth laser pistol (item level 1, 1d4 F, 80-foot range increment), azimuth laser rifle (1d8 F)
  • Best For: Precision shooters who want reach
Plasma Weapons

Superheated matter that deals electricity and fire damage together (E & F).

  • Advantages: High damage dice, and many plasma weapons have strong critical effects (burn, severe wound)
  • Disadvantages: Higher item levels and prices; plasma pistols have short range increments
  • Examples: Red star plasma pistol (item level 7, 1d8 E & F), plasma swords
  • Best For: Heavy damage dealers who want maximum firepower
Shock Weapons

Electrical discharges that deal electricity (E) damage.

  • Advantages: Many have the arc critical effect, so a critical hit also zaps a second creature nearby
  • Disadvantages: Some creatures and robots resist electricity
  • Examples: Static arc pistol (item level 2, 1d6 E), arc rifles, shock truncheons
  • Best For: Characters who like chaining damage through tight groups

Sonic (So) and cryo (C) weapons round out the energy options. Some weapons also have the stun property, which you can switch on as a move action to make all their attacks nonlethal.

Choosing Your Primary Weapon

Class-Based Weapon Recommendations

Soldier - The Weapon Master

Primary Focus: Maximize damage output and tactical flexibility

  • Early Game: Choose weapons that match your fighting style specialization
  • Mid Game: Invest in high-quality versions of your preferred weapon type
  • Late Game: Collect multiple specialized weapons for different situations
  • Recommended Types: Soldiers are proficient with every weapon category (basic and advanced melee, small arms, longarms, heavy weapons, sniper weapons, grenades) and with light and heavy armor, so let your fighting style decide
Operative - The Precision Striker

Primary Focus: Weapons that work well with trick attack and stealth

  • Proficiencies (Core Rulebook): Basic melee weapons, small arms and sniper weapons; light armor
  • Preferred Weapons: Weapons with the operative property, which work with trick attack
  • Key Features: High accuracy, good critical hit potential, concealable
  • Avoid: Heavy weapons, area effect weapons, obvious military hardware
  • Special Consideration: Quiet, concealable gear that suits stealth
Technomancer - The Magical Marksman

Primary Focus: Simple, reliable weapons that don't interfere with spellcasting

  • Proficiencies (Core Rulebook): Basic melee weapons and small arms; light armor
  • Key Features: A reliable small arm; armor never interferes with Starfinder spellcasting, so pick light armor for its defenses
  • Synergy Options: Weapons that can be enhanced with technomancer spells
  • Backup Plan: Always have a reliable ranged weapon for when spells run out
Solarian - The Stellar Warrior

Primary Focus: Weapons that complement solar manifestations without redundancy

  • Solar Weapon Users: Backup ranged weapons for when melee isn't viable
  • Solar Armor Users: Balanced weapon selection for versatile combat roles
  • Proficiencies (Core Rulebook): Basic and advanced melee weapons and small arms; light armor
  • Philosophy: Weapons should harmonize with cosmic powers rather than replace them

Understanding Weapon Properties

Beyond basic damage, weapons have special properties that dramatically affect their utility:

Damage Properties

  • Automatic: When you full attack, you can spray a cone instead, rolling an attack against each creature in it; it burns ammunition fast
  • Blast: Fires a cone out to the first range increment; roll an attack against each target in the cone
  • Explode: Aim at a grid intersection; everything in the radius takes the listed damage (Reflex save for half)
  • Penetrating: Ignores hardness equal to the weapon's item level, for breaking through doors, walls and objects

Utility Properties

  • Bright: Raises the light level within 20 feet of your target by one step for 1 round
  • Stun: Switch to stun mode as a move action, and all attacks deal nonlethal damage
  • Trip: +2 to your attack roll when you attempt a trip combat maneuver
  • Disarm: +2 to your attack roll when you attempt a disarm combat maneuver

Enhancement Properties

  • Boost: Charge the weapon as a move action to add the listed damage to your next attack (costs extra charges)
  • Quick Reload: Reload as part of the same action as firing instead of spending a move action
  • Unwieldy: Can't be used in a full attack or any other action that makes multiple attacks
  • Operative: Works with an operative's trick attack, and anyone can add Dexterity instead of Strength to melee attack rolls with it

Critical Hits and Critical Effects

A natural 20 on an attack roll always hits. If the total also meets or beats the target's EAC or KAC, it's a critical hit: roll damage twice (with all your bonuses each time) and add the rolls together. There is no separate confirmation roll. Many weapons also list a critical effect that triggers on a critical hit; any save it allows uses DC 10 + ½ item level + your ability modifier.

  • Arc: The energy leaps to the nearest other creature within 10 feet for the listed damage
  • Bleed / Burn / Corrode: The target gains the bleeding or burning condition (corrode works like burning but deals acid damage)
  • Knockdown: The target is knocked prone
  • Staggered / Deafen: The target must make a Fortitude save or be staggered for 1 round, or deafened for 1d4 minutes
  • Wound / Severe Wound: Roll on the wounding table (twice and choose, for severe wound)

Armor - Your Second Skin

The Armor Equation

Starfinder armor isn't just about stopping damage – it's a complex system that balances protection, mobility, and functionality. Think of armor like a car: you want safety features, but you also need performance, comfort, and the right features for your driving style.

Light Armor - The Agile Option

Philosophy: "Don't Get Hit"

Light armor focuses on mobility and stealth over raw protection. It's designed for characters who avoid damage through speed, stealth, and positioning rather than absorbing hits.

Characteristics
  • EAC and KAC Bonuses: Separate bonuses that rise with item level, from +1 EAC/+2 KAC for a level-1 second skin to around +20 at the top levels
  • Max Dex Bonus: High (roughly +3 to +8)
  • Armor Check Penalty: None or −1
  • Speed Adjustment: None
  • Upgrade Slots: 0 at low levels, more as item level rises
  • Donning: 4 rounds to put on or take off
Light Armor Examples
  • Second Skin: Ultra-thin protection for stealth operations
  • Stationwear: Fashionable armor for urban environments
  • D-Suit: Exploration gear for dangerous environments
  • Carbon Skin: Graphite to nanotube grades of synthetic protection
  • Kasatha Microcord: Woven armor from kasathan tradition, made in several grades
Ideal For
  • Operatives: Who need stealth and mobility
  • Technomancers and mystics: Light armor is all their class grants, and it never hinders spellcasting
  • Envoys: Who balance combat with social roles
  • High-Dex characters: Who want to maximize their agility bonus

Heavy Armor - The Tank Option

Philosophy: "Absorb Everything"

Heavy armor maximizes protection at the cost of mobility. It's for characters who plan to stand in the line of fire and absorb punishment while dealing damage or protecting allies.

Characteristics
  • EAC and KAC Bonuses: Higher than light armor of the same level; a level-1 golemforged plating gives +2 EAC/+5 KAC
  • Max Dex Bonus: Low (roughly +0 to +4)
  • Armor Check Penalty: Significant (about −2 to −5)
  • Speed Adjustment: −5 or −10 feet
  • Upgrade Slots: More than light armor of the same level
  • Donning: 16 rounds to put on or take off; you need light armor proficiency plus heavy armor proficiency (soldiers have it; others take the Heavy Armor Proficiency feat)
Heavy Armor Examples
  • Golemforged Plating: Massive protection for front-line fighters, available from 1st level
  • Vesk Overplate: Heavy plate in the vesk military tradition
  • Aegis Series: High-level heavy armor for elite troops
Ideal For
  • Soldiers: Who specialize in front-line combat
  • Solarians: Only with the Heavy Armor Proficiency feat, since the class grants light armor
  • Tank-focused characters: Who draw enemy fire
  • Low-Dex characters: Whose low Dexterity modifier already fits under a heavy armor's Max Dex

Powered Armor - The Best of Both Worlds

Philosophy: "Technology Solves Everything"

Powered armor is a battery-driven frame with its own Strength score, speed and weapon slots. It's expensive and complex, but offers capabilities that regular armor can't match. The Core Rulebook's battle harness, for example, is item level 5: +9 EAC, +12 KAC, Strength 18, speed 30 feet, a battery that lasts 20 hours.

Characteristics
  • EAC and KAC Bonuses: High, from +9/+12 (battle harness, level 5) to +18/+24 (jarlslayer, level 15)
  • Max Dex Bonus: Low (the battle harness allows +2)
  • Armor Check Penalty: Significant (−4 on the battle harness)
  • Speed: The suit has its own speed instead of an adjustment
  • Special Features: Its own Strength (which also sets your carrying capacity), weapon slots and upgrade slots
Special Powered Armor Features
  • Strength Score: The suit's Strength replaces yours, for melee and carrying
  • Environmental Protection: Like all armor, it shields you from vacuum and hostile atmospheres
  • Built-in Attack: Each suit lists its own unarmed damage (1d10 B for the battle harness)
  • Weapon Slots: Mount weapons on the frame
  • Upgrade Slots: Take armor upgrades like any other armor
Powered Armor Examples
  • Battle Harness: The basic infantry frame used by professional militaries
  • Jarlslayer: High-level assault armor
  • Later books add many more frames, from cargo lifters to flight frames.
Powered Armor Considerations
  • Cost: Extremely expensive to purchase and maintain
  • Power Source: Uses standard batteries; recharge it at a generator or recharging station, or swap in a new battery
  • Complexity: Can malfunction or be disabled by EMP effects
  • Proficiency: Requires powered armor proficiency, which most characters gain through a feat
  • Social Impact: May be restricted or cause alarm in civilian areas

Armor Upgrades - Customization Options

Each suit of armor has a number of upgrade slots; each upgrade has its own item level and fills one or more slots. Some examples:

Defensive Upgrades

  • Force Field: An energy shield in several colour grades (brown at level 3 up to prismatic at 20)
  • Radiation Buffer: Extra protection against radiation
  • Thermal Capacitor: Extra protection against extreme temperatures
  • Jump Jets: Short bursts of flight (light or heavy armor)

Utility Upgrades

  • Infrared Sensors: Sensors that pick up heat signatures in the dark
  • Filtered Rebreather: Extra help against airborne hazards
  • No upgrade is needed for basic life support: every suit of armor already has environmental protections.

Combat Upgrades

  • Targeting Computer: Computer-assisted aim
  • Weapon Slots: On powered armor, mount weapons directly on the frame

Equipment Economics and Progression

Understanding Wealth by Level

The Core Rulebook gives GMs a table of how much total wealth (gear plus credits) a character is expected to have at each level, from 1,000 credits at 1st level to 3,775,000 at 20th. GMs use it to pace rewards and to equip characters who start above 1st level. Think of it like a video game where you unlock better gear as you progress.

Understanding the Progression

Notice how wealth grows roughly geometrically: about 50% more each level from the middle levels on (100,000 at 11th, 150,000 at 12th, 225,000 at 13th). Item prices climb the same way, so a weapon a few levels behind you quickly stops pulling its weight.

For a character built above 1st level, the Core Rulebook suggests spending no more than half your wealth on any single item; for a balanced build, no more than 35% on any one weapon and 35% on armor and any one protective device.

Equipment Spending Priorities

Not all equipment is created equal. Smart spending means prioritizing items that have the biggest impact on your character's effectiveness. The order below is this guide's advice, not a rule.

Priority 1: Survival Essentials

Equipment that keeps you alive and functional in basic situations.

  • Primary Weapon: Your main damage-dealing tool
  • Armor: Protection appropriate for your role
  • Basic Medical Supplies: Healing items for emergency recovery
  • Environmental Gear: Supplies for places where your armor's built-in protections aren't enough

Priority 2: Professional Tools

Equipment that makes you effective at your chosen role and class abilities.

  • Professional Kits: Tools that give bonuses to your key skills
  • Secondary Weapons: Backup options for different situations
  • Technology: Computers, vehicles, and utility devices
  • Armor Upgrades: Customizations that enhance your protection

Priority 3: Force Multipliers

Equipment that amplifies your existing capabilities beyond baseline effectiveness.

  • Weapon Fusions: Magical add-ons such as flaming; the total level of a weapon's fusions can't exceed the weapon's item level, and a fusion seal lets you move a fusion to a new weapon
  • Augmentations: Permanent improvements to your body
  • Magic Items: Technological or mystical enhancement devices
  • Advanced Consumables: High-quality ammunition, medical supplies, and specialty items

Priority 4: Specialty Gear

Situational equipment for specific challenges or character concepts.

  • Niche Tools: Specialized equipment for unusual situations
  • Luxury Items: Status symbols and comfort improvements
  • Experimental Gear: Cutting-edge technology with unknown reliability
  • Collection Items: Equipment that supports character themes and interests

When to Upgrade Equipment

Level-Based Upgrades

Certain levels represent major equipment upgrade opportunities:

  • Every level: Expected wealth rises each level (for example, 9,000 credits at 5th and 15,000 at 6th), so check what new item levels you can now afford
  • When a weapon falls behind: Compare its item level to your character level; one several levels lower is usually due for replacement
  • Major Story Beats: Successful missions often provide equipment rewards
  • New Environments: Entering different campaign phases may require different gear

Performance-Based Upgrades

Replace equipment when it's no longer meeting your needs:

  • Missing Too Often: When your attack bonuses fall behind enemy defenses
  • Insufficient Damage: When your weapons aren't threatening to enemies
  • Getting Hit Too Much: When your AC can't keep up with enemy accuracy
  • Failing Skill Checks: When your tools aren't providing adequate bonuses

Opportunity-Based Upgrades

Sometimes upgrade opportunities present themselves:

  • Found Equipment: Loot from defeated enemies or discovered caches
  • Mission Rewards: Equipment given as payment for successful missions
  • Crafting Opportunities: When you have time and resources to build custom gear
  • Shopping Expeditions: When visiting major trade centers with good selection

Activity 2: Weapon Selection Matrix

Create a decision matrix for choosing between different weapon options:

  1. Choose a character class and level
  2. Identify 3-4 weapon options within budget
  3. Rate each weapon (1-5) on these criteria:
    • Damage output potential
    • Accuracy and reliability
    • Versatility (multiple situations)
    • Ammunition/maintenance cost
    • Character concept fit
  4. Calculate total scores and justify your final choice

Activity 3: Armor Optimization Challenge

Given these constraints, optimize an armor loadout:

  • Character: 5th level Operative (Dex 18)
  • Budget: 3,150 credits for armor and upgrades (35% of the 9,000 credits a 5th-level character is expected to have)
  • Mission: Corporate infiltration with potential combat
  • Requirements: Stealth capability, social acceptability, emergency protection

Choose armor type, upgrades, and justify how the combination meets all requirements. Hint: with Dexterity 18 (+4), look for light armor whose Max Dex Bonus is at least +4, and remember that operatives are proficient only with light armor.

Activity 4: Technology Integration Exercise

Design a technology suite for a specific character role:

  2. Select appropriate computers, tools, and devices
  3. Plan how the technologies work together
  4. Identify potential failure points and backup systems
  5. Calculate total cost and maintenance requirements

Common Equipment Problems and Solutions

Problem: "I can't afford the gear I want"

Solutions:

  • Prioritize ruthlessly: Buy essentials first, luxuries later
  • Consider lower-level gear: A cheaper, lower-item-level version of the same weapon or armor line
  • Team coordination: Share expensive equipment between party members
  • Crafting: Building gear from UPBs doesn't save credits in Starfinder, but it lets you make exactly what you need when no shop has it
  • Mission rewards: Choose missions that provide needed equipment

Problem: "My equipment is becoming obsolete"

Solutions:

  • Regular upgrade schedule: Budget for equipment replacement
  • Modification over replacement: Upgrade existing gear when possible
  • Sell old gear: Expect only about 10% of its price, so selling is a top-up, not a plan
  • Repurpose equipment: Find new uses for old gear
  • Emergency backup: Keep older gear as backup systems

Problem: "I have too much gear to carry"

Solutions:

  • Bulk optimization: Choose items with L or negligible Bulk where possible
  • Storage solutions: Your starship's hold, vehicles, and base storage
  • Team distribution: Spread equipment across party members
  • Mission-specific loadouts: Carry only what you need for each mission
  • Equipment caches: Pre-position gear at common destinations

Problem: "My equipment keeps breaking"

Solutions:

  • Quality investment: Buy higher-grade equipment that lasts longer
  • Redundant systems: Always have backups for critical equipment
  • Proper maintenance: Regular care extends equipment life
  • Environmental protection: Shield gear from harsh conditions
  • Repair skills: Learn to fix your own equipment

The Equipment Mindset

Experienced players understand that gear is a tool in service of character concept and story. The best equipment isn't necessarily the most expensive or most powerful – it's the gear that helps you tell the story you want to tell and solve the problems you're likely to face.

Equipment Wisdom for Starfinder Heroes

  • "Failure to prepare is preparing to fail" – Always consider what could go wrong
  • "The right tool for the job" – Match equipment to expected challenges
  • "Backup systems save lives" – Redundancy prevents single points of failure
  • "Function over form" – Effectiveness matters more than appearance
  • "Adapt and overcome" – Creative use of equipment solves unexpected problems

The Most Important Equipment Rule

Remember that equipment serves the story, not the other way around. The most elaborate gear list in the galaxy won't save a character who doesn't fit the campaign or whose player isn't engaged with the story. Choose equipment that enhances your character's capabilities and supports their role in the party, but never forget that creativity, teamwork, and good roleplay are the most powerful tools in any adventurer's kit.

Your equipment will change as your character grows and faces new challenges. Stay flexible, keep learning, and always be ready to adapt when the universe throws something unexpected at you. After all, the best equipment is the gear you have with you when you need it most!
